## 2. Game design

**One line.** You are the city's traffic commissioner. Each turn you play rule cards onto a live street grid, press Go, and watch 15 seconds of simulated rush hour. Move enough cars through before the grid locks, or the run ends.

**Design pillars (every feature is judged against these, in order)**

1. Legible cause and effect. A player must be able to point at a jam and name the card that caused or cured it. No hidden modifiers.
2. One decision per 10 seconds. Draft, place, Go. No menus inside the loop.
3. Combos are the content. 40 cards must produce at least 5 archetypes that feel different to pilot; the sim is the content generator, not hand-made levels.
4. Runs end. 12 rounds, 10–15 minutes, then a result screen and a reason to go again (new mayor, new daily seed, one more district).
5. Watchable. The 15-second resolution phase must be fun to watch with no input and must clip well for a 9:16 video.

**Core loop (30 seconds)**

1. Draw phase: hand of 5 rule cards drawn from the run deck (starting deck 12 cards).
2. Place phase: play up to 2 cards onto intersections, road segments, or the whole district (card decides the target type). Energy: 3 per round; cards cost 1–3. Unplayed cards return to the deck (no discard pile at launch; keeps the mental model small).
3. Go phase: 15 seconds of simulation at 1× (player may hold to run 2×). Vehicles spawn at edges per the round's demand profile, path to exits, and interact with the rules.
4. Score phase: throughput (vehicles exited) is compared to the round quota. Pass: earn funds and pick 1 of 3 offered cards to add to the deck (or skip for +2 funds). Fail: lose 1 of 3 commissioner approval points. Zero approval ends the run.
5. Every 4 rounds a shop appears: spend funds to buy a card, remove a card, or upgrade a card (one tier, section 4).

**Run structure (30 days)**

- A run is 12 rounds across one district. Rounds 4, 8 and 12 are "rush rounds" with 1.6× demand and a boss condition (section 5).
- Winning a run on a district unlocks the next district and awards mayor XP. Three districts at launch (free: District 1; unlock: Districts 2–3, all mayors beyond the first two, daily mode after 3 free days, and run history).
- Mayors are starting-deck and passive variants (section 5). Six at launch.
- Daily seed: one seeded run per day, identical for everyone, with a leaderboard and a share card.
- Endless mode after the first District 3 win: rounds continue with demand +8% per round until a fail; leaderboard by round reached.

**Scoring and quotas**

- Throughput is the only score. Quota for round r in district d: `quota = base[d] * (1 + 0.11*(r-1)) * rushMult`, base = \[26, 34, 42\], rushMult = 1.6 on rush rounds, else 1. Values are starting points; the balance simulation (section 11) tunes them so that the median unupgraded starting deck fails at round 6–7 of District 1 and the median well-drafted deck wins District 1 at a 55–65% rate.
- Gridlock: if no vehicle moves for 3 consecutive sim seconds, the round ends immediately with the current throughput; this is the "boss" feeling and must be dramatic (section 7).
- Funds: +4 per passed round, +1 per 10% over quota (max +6), shop prices 5–12.

**Difficulty and fairness rules**

- Demand profiles are seeded and shown before placement as a 15-second heat-strip per edge, so the player can plan. Nothing is random after Go.
- Card offers use a pity system: at least one card of the player's dominant archetype tag in every 3-card offer after round 3.
- First run ever is a scripted 4-round tutorial district with fixed draws (section 8); it is skippable and never shown again.
- No timers during placement. Players may inspect any intersection and preview a card's effect before committing (ghost preview, section 8).

**What this game is not**

- Not a city builder: no road building, no zoning. The map is fixed per round.
- Not a real-time strategy: no input during Go except pause and speed.
- Not a sandbox: every run has a win and a loss condition.

## 3. Traffic simulation specification

The sim is a discrete-time, cell-based model (Nagel–Schreckenberg style on a grid of lanes), not a continuous physics sim. Cell-based keeps it deterministic, cheap (thousands of runs per second headless), and legible. Implement it in `packages/sim` with zero rendering dependencies.

**World**

- Grid of 7×9 tiles (portrait). Tiles are road or block. Roads form a connected graph; each road tile has 1–2 lanes per direction, each lane is 4 cells long per tile.
- Intersections are tiles where 3–4 road tiles meet. Each has a controller: `uncontrolled` (default, right-of-way rules), `signal` (phases), `roundabout`, `stop-all`, or `priority(dir)`. Cards change controllers and parameters.
- Edges: spawners and sinks on the map boundary. Each round's demand profile is a list of (edge, time, vehicleType, destinationEdge) tuples generated from the seed before Go and shown to the player.
- Map generation: seeded, per district, from a template bank of 12 layouts per district with mirrored and rotated variants; District 1 maps have 4–6 intersections, District 3 maps 9–12.

**Vehicles**

| Type | Length (cells) | Max speed (cells/tick) | Behavior | Score value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Car | 1 | 3 | Standard | 1 |
| Taxi | 1 | 3 | Prefers shortest-time route, re-routes at each intersection | 1 |
| Bus | 2 | 2 | Stops 4 ticks at bus-stop tiles; counts 3 | 3 |
| Truck | 3 | 2 | Cannot use "no heavy" roads; blocks a full intersection cell while turning | 2 |
| Emergency | 1 | 4 | Others yield; ignores signals; appears only on rush rounds | 5 |
| Bike | 1 | 2 | Uses bike lanes if present, else shares the lane at speed 1 | 1 |

**Tick rules (order matters; all vehicles update in a fixed deterministic order each tick, sorted by (laneId, cellIndex) descending so downstream moves first)**

1. Acceleration: `v = min(v+1, vmax)`.
2. Gap: `v = min(v, gapAhead)` where gap accounts for the next vehicle, an intersection stop line when the controller says stop, and a turn-reservation cell.
3. Randomization: none by default. The classic NaSch slowdown probability exists as a parameter `p_slow` that cards can raise (e.g. "Fog") and is driven by the seeded PRNG only, never `Math.random`.
4. Move: advance `v` cells; on crossing an intersection, follow the pre-computed route (BFS shortest path at spawn; taxis recompute on entry using time-weighted costs).
5. Intersection controllers resolve conflicts: signals by phase; uncontrolled by "first at line, then rightmost"; roundabouts by "inside has priority, max occupancy 4"; priority(dir) by direction.
6. Exit: a vehicle entering a sink is removed and adds its score value to throughput.

**Timing**

- 20 ticks per sim second; a round is 300 ticks. Rendering interpolates between ticks (section 6). 2× speed runs 40 ticks per render second, identical results.
- Gridlock detector: if the sum of `v` across all vehicles is 0 for 60 consecutive ticks, the round ends.

**Determinism contract**

- `simulate(seed, mapId, demandProfile, placements, cardState) -> RoundResult` is a pure function. `RoundResult` includes throughput, per-tick vehicle positions (optional, for replay), and a 64-bit hash of the final state.
- The PRNG is a seeded xoshiro128\*\* (or equivalent) owned by the round; no other randomness anywhere in `packages/sim`.
- Floating point is banned in `packages/sim`; all quantities are integers. Any rounding happens in the renderer.
- A golden-file test runs 200 seeded rounds and compares hashes on every CI run on Node, Chromium, WebKit (Playwright) and the Android/iOS WebViews (Appium or Capacitor test harness). A hash mismatch fails the build.

**Performance budget**

- Headless: at least 2,000 full 300-tick rounds per second on a developer laptop single core, with up to 150 vehicles. This is what makes the balance simulation (section 11) feasible.
- Rendered: the sim step runs in a Web Worker; the main thread only draws. Sim and render never share mutable state; the worker posts compact typed-array snapshots per tick.

## 4. Card system

Cards are data, not code: every card is a JSON entry in `packages/content/cards/*.json` validated by a schema, with effects expressed as a small set of primitives the sim understands. New cards must never require a sim change; if one does, the primitive is added to the sim with tests first.

**Card anatomy**

- `id`, `name`, `cost` (1–3 energy), `target` (`intersection` | `segment` | `edge` | `district`), `duration` (`round` | `run`), `rarity` (`common` 60% / `uncommon` 30% / `rare` 10% of offers), `tags` (archetype tags below), `effects[]` (primitives), `upgrade` (the tier-2 version's deltas), `flavor` (one line, max 60 chars).
- Effect primitives (initial set, extend only with tests): `setController`, `setSignalPhase`, `setLaneCount`, `setSpeedLimit`, `banType`, `preferType`, `addBusStop`, `addBikeLane`, `setOneWay`, `reroute`, `spawnModifier`, `scoreModifier`, `setPSlow`, `yieldRule`, `drawModifier`, `energyModifier`, `fundsModifier`.
- Stacking: same card on same target does not stack; different cards do. Conflicting controllers: the last played wins and the earlier card is refunded.

**Archetype tags (the design target is that each is viable and feels different)**

| Tag | Fantasy | Wins by | Signature synergy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FLOW | Smooth signals and one-ways | Raising average speed | Green Wave + One-Way Pair |
| MASS | Buses and bikes | High score per vehicle | Bus Priority + Express Stop + Bike Boulevard |
| TOLL | Pricing and rerouting | Shaping demand before it arrives | Congestion Charge + Detour + Park & Ride |
| CHAOS | Roundabouts and uncontrolled junctions | Throughput with risk; punishes trucks | Roundabout Ring + Yield Culture + No Heavy |
| TEMPO | Energy, draw, and tempo | Playing more cards per round | Overtime + Second Shift + Grant |

**Launch card list (40). Format: name (cost, target, rarity, tags): effect → upgrade**

1. Green Wave (2, segment, C, FLOW): signals along the segment share phase offsets tuned to speed limit → +1 segment length.
2. Smart Signal (1, intersection, C, FLOW): controller = signal with adaptive phase (longest queue gets green) → phase switch 2 ticks faster.
3. One-Way (1, segment, C, FLOW): segment becomes one-way in the chosen direction, lanes doubled that way → cost 0.
4. One-Way Pair (2, district, U, FLOW): all parallel segments alternate one-way directions → also +1 speed on them.
5. Speed Limit Up (1, segment, C, FLOW): vmax +1 on segment → +1 for all types including trucks.
6. Turn Lane (1, intersection, C, FLOW): adds a dedicated turn reservation cell; turns no longer block through traffic → both turn directions.
7. Grid Sync (3, district, R, FLOW): all signals share a district-wide cycle; +2 throughput score per 10 vehicles exited in the same tick window → bonus doubles.
8. Bus Priority (1, intersection, C, MASS): buses get green on approach → also taxis.
9. Express Stop (1, segment, C, MASS): bus stop dwell 4 → 2 ticks on the segment → dwell 1.
10. Bus Lane (2, segment, U, MASS): one lane reserved for buses and bikes; cars lose it → bikes reach bus speed.
11. Bike Boulevard (2, segment, U, MASS): bike lane added; bikes vmax 3; cars vmax −1 → cars unaffected.
12. Articulated Fleet (2, district, U, MASS): buses length 3 and value 5 → value 6.
13. Transit Hub (3, intersection, R, MASS): intersection becomes a sink for buses (they count on arrival) → also taxis.
14. Fare Holiday (1, district, C, MASS): +40% bus and bike spawns this round, −20% cars → +60%/−30%.
15. Congestion Charge (2, district, U, TOLL): cars entering the central third pay: 25% reroute to the ring, +1 funds per 5 charged → +1 funds per 3.
16. Detour (1, edge, C, TOLL): 50% of spawns from this edge rerouted to the adjacent edge → player picks any edge.
17. Park & Ride (2, edge, U, TOLL): 30% of car spawns at this edge convert to bus riders (removed, +1 score each) → 50%.
18. Toll Booth (1, segment, C, TOLL): vehicles slow to 1 for 2 cells; +1 funds per 4 → no slowdown.
19. Staggered Hours (2, district, U, TOLL): demand profile spread: peak reduced 30%, spread over the round → 40%.
20. Delivery Window (1, district, C, TOLL): trucks spawn only in the first 5 seconds → trucks value +1.
21. Dynamic Pricing (3, district, R, TOLL): every time an intersection queue exceeds 6, 2 waiting cars despawn with +1 score ("took the train") → queue threshold 4.
22. Roundabout (1, intersection, C, CHAOS): controller = roundabout → capacity 6.
23. Roundabout Ring (3, district, R, CHAOS): all 4-way intersections become roundabouts; +1 vmax inside → +2.
24. Yield Culture (2, district, U, CHAOS): uncontrolled intersections resolve 1 tick faster → 2 ticks.
25. No Heavy (1, segment, C, CHAOS): trucks banned from the segment (reroute at spawn) → district-wide for one road class.
26. Remove Signal (1, intersection, C, CHAOS): controller = uncontrolled; refund 1 energy if it was a signal → refund 2.
27. Shared Space (2, intersection, U, CHAOS): all types vmax 1 inside but no stopping ever; capacity unlimited → vmax 2.
28. Fog (1, district, C, CHAOS): p\_slow +10% for everyone; +3 score per exited vehicle above quota → +4.
29. Emergency Corridor (2, segment, U, CHAOS): emergency vehicles at vmax 5 and never blocked; each exit +3 extra → +5.
30. Overtime (1, district, C, TEMPO): +1 energy next round → +2.
31. Second Shift (2, district, U, TEMPO): draw 2 extra cards next round → and +1 energy.
32. Grant (1, district, C, TEMPO): +5 funds → +8.
33. Pilot Program (1, district, C, TEMPO): the next card played this round costs 0 → next two.
34. Consultants (2, district, U, TEMPO): offer 4 cards instead of 3 for the rest of the run → 5.
35. Infrastructure Bond (3, district, R, TEMPO): −2 funds per round for the run; +1 energy per round for the run → −1 funds.
36. Recount (1, district, C, TEMPO): redraw your hand → keep 1 first.
37. Public Hearing (2, district, U, TEMPO): remove a card from your deck permanently; +3 funds → +5.
38. Ring Road (3, district, R, FLOW/TOLL): the outer loop gets +1 lane and vmax +1 both ways → also one-way optimized by demand.
39. School Zone (1, segment, C, MASS/CHAOS): vmax 1 on segment; bikes value 2 while here → value 3.
40. Commissioner's Decree (3, district, R, any): copy the effect of the last card played, at no cost, for the rest of the run → also upgrades it.

**Draft and deck rules**

- Starting deck (default mayor): 2× Smart Signal, 2× One-Way, 1× Roundabout, 1× Bus Priority, 1× Detour, 1× Overtime, 1× Grant, 1× Turn Lane, 1× Speed Limit Up, 1× Recount (12 cards).
- Offers after a passed round: 3 cards, rarity by the weights above, pity rule from section 2, no duplicates of a `run`-duration card already in the deck.
- Upgrades: the shop upgrades one card to tier 2 for 6 funds; tier-2 is the `upgrade` delta; no tier 3 at launch.
- Hand size 5, energy 3, max 2 cards played per round, deck shuffles when empty; all of these are mayor-modifiable.

**Content pipeline rules for agents**

- Adding or changing a card requires: schema validation, a unit test of each primitive used, and a balance report (section 11) attached to the PR showing the card's drafted win-rate delta.
- Card text is generated from the effect data by a template, never hand-written, so the text can never lie about the effect. Flavor text is the only free-form field.
- Every card has a 48×48 procedural glyph generated from its primitives (section 7), so visual identity is also derived from data.

**Meta-progression (deliberately thin; the run is the content)**

- Mayor XP from any run: 1 per round passed, 5 per district won. Levels 1–5 per mayor unlock cosmetic liveries only (card-back pattern, signal color set, vehicle palette). No power.
- Codex: every card seen is recorded with the player's win rate when drafted; this is the in-game stats page and doubles as the hook for players to try the other archetypes.
- Run history: last 50 runs with seed, mayor, result, deck (unlock feature). Any run can be replayed from history (deterministic sim).

**Daily seed**

- One run per calendar day (UTC rollover shown as a countdown) with a fixed mayor, district, map, demand, and card offers; identical for every player.
- Playable once; result is final. The leaderboard is score (throughput total), then rounds survived, then run time.
- Free players get the daily for 3 days, then the daily becomes part of the unlock; the share card remains viewable.
- Leaderboard is anonymous (random handle generated locally, editable, profanity-filtered), submitted with the run's final-state hash; the backend re-simulates a random 5% of submissions to catch tampering (section 10).

**Share card**

- Generated client-side as PNG (1080×1920 and 1200×630) and as a text block, like Wordle's grid: title, day number, district, 12 round glyphs (green = passed, amber = passed under 110%, red = failed, black = gridlock), total throughput, mayor glyph, and the URL.
- The text version must be legible as plain text in a chat: `Gridlock Royale #212 · Midtown · 🟩🟩🟨🟩🟩🟩🟥🟩🟩🟩🟩⬛ · 412 through` plus the link.
- Share uses the Web Share API, the native share sheet on Capacitor, and clipboard fallback. Also a 6-second MP4/GIF of the final rush round rendered client-side from the replay, for TikTok/Shorts (desktop and mobile; web portals get the PNG only to stay under budget).

**Key interfaces (write these first; everything else codes against them)**

- `Sim.simulate(input: RoundInput): RoundResult` and `Sim.step(state): state` (for the worker's tick-by-tick mode).
- `RoundInput = { seed, mapId, demandProfile, placements: Placement[], cardState: CardState, districtRules }`.
- `PortalAdapter = { init, gameplayStart, gameplayStop, commercialBreak (no-op for us but required by SDKs), happyTime, shareUrl }`.
- `Entitlement = { isUnlocked(): boolean, purchase(): Promise<Result>, restore(): Promise<Result>, source: 'steam'|'ios'|'android'|'web'|'none' }`.
- `SaveStore` with versioned, migratable JSON; written atomically; storage backends: localStorage/IndexedDB (web), Capacitor Preferences + Filesystem (mobile), app data dir (desktop), optional anonymous cloud sync keyed by a locally generated UUID.

**Determinism and the worker**

- The sim runs in a Web Worker (or a Node worker thread on desktop). The main thread sends `RoundInput` and receives per-tick `Uint16Array` snapshots (vehicle id, lane, cell, speed, type) via transferable buffers.
- The renderer interpolates between the last two snapshots at the display refresh rate; it never predicts.
- Replay = re-run the sim from `RoundInput`; nothing is recorded but inputs. Save files store inputs only.

**Budgets (enforced in CI; a regression fails the build)**

| Budget | Limit |

## 7. Art and audio direction

**Visual identity: "transit map comes alive."** Flat, high-contrast, geometric, like a metro diagram that started moving. Everything is generated in code from a palette and a shape grammar; there are no image files in the repo except generated atlases that CI rebuilds from source.

**Rules**

- Top-down orthographic. Roads are rounded rectangles with a 2-px lane divider; intersections are squares with controller glyphs in the center (signal = three dots, roundabout = ring, stop = octagon, priority = chevron).
- Vehicles are rounded rectangles sized by length with a single accent stripe by type: car (palette accent), taxi (yellow stripe), bus (long, two windows as lighter rectangles), truck (cab + box, darker), emergency (white with a blinking 2-frame beacon), bike (circle + short tail). Every vehicle is readable at 12 px.
- Motion feedback: speed is shown by a 1–3-cell trail with alpha fade; queuing vehicles pulse their outline once per second; gridlock turns the whole queue a desaturated red and shakes the camera 4 px for 300 ms.
- Card glyphs: 48×48 generated from the card's primitives (controller glyph + target shape + tag color); tier-2 cards get a gold border and a second ring. Card frames are flat with a 2-px tag-colored edge.
- Districts: one base palette each (Old Town warm stone/terracotta; Midtown cool greys/teal; Interchange navy/neon magenta), generated as 5 tints and 1 accent from two seed hues via OKLCH, validated for WCAG AA contrast between vehicles and road in both light and dark variants.
- Typography: one open-licensed geometric sans (e.g. Inter or Space Grotesk, OFL) rendered to a bitmap atlas by the tooling for Pixi text; DOM menus use the same font via CSS. Numbers tabular.
- UI motion: 120–200 ms ease-out for everything; no bounce; a card placed snaps with a 2-frame scale pop.
- Deliberately absent: faces, characters, illustrations, gradients heavier than 10% luminance, particle fountains. The mayors are glyph badges with a title, not portraits.

**Accessibility in the art**

- Every vehicle type and controller is distinguishable by shape alone, not color; a colorblind-safe palette toggle swaps accents (deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia sets validated with a simulator in CI).
- Minimum 44-px touch targets; HUD text 14 px minimum on phone.

**Audio: synthesized, procedural, quiet.** No audio files. SFX are parameter sets for an in-repo zzfx-class synthesizer; music is a step sequencer with per-district scales.

- SFX set (each a parameter object with 2–3 seeded variants to avoid machine-gun repetition): card draw, card hover, card place (per tag pitch), card refund, Go button, round tick (soft, every sim second), vehicle exit (a short pluck, pitch by score value), queue building (low hum that rises with queue length, capped), gridlock (descending chord + thud), quota pass, quota fail, shop buy, upgrade, unlock purchase, share.
- Music: ambient generative loops at 80–100 BPM; District 1 pentatonic marimba, District 2 minor-7 pads with a light hi-hat, District 3 synthwave bass. Intensity rises with queue load (filter cutoff), drops on gridlock. All generated at runtime; total code under 40 KB.
- Mixer: master, music, SFX sliders; mute on background; haptics on mobile for card place and gridlock (Capacitor Haptics).

**Tooling deliverables**

- `tools/sprites`: builds the atlas from the shape grammar; deterministic output; runs in CI and fails on diff unless the PR says "art change."
- `tools/store-assets`: renders icons (all required sizes), feature graphics, and screenshot frames for every store from live game scenes with localized captions.
- A style page at `/dev/style` in the web build that shows every generated asset side by side for review by Idan.

## 8. UX, screens, input, onboarding, accessibility, localization

**Screens (all portrait-first; landscape rearranges HUD to the right)**

1. Title: play (continue if a run exists), daily, mayors, codex, settings, unlock badge or "owned" check. One tap to play.
2. Mayor select: 6 badges, locked ones show the unlock CTA.
3. Run screen: map fills the top 70%; demand heat-strips on the edges; hand of 5 cards in a fan at the bottom; energy pips and round/quota at the top; Go button bottom-right. Tap a card to see a ghost of its effect on valid targets; tap a target to place; tap again to undo before Go.
4. Resolution: map plays; HUD shows throughput vs quota as a filling bar; hold anywhere for 2×; pause button.
5. Round result: pass/fail, funds, 3-card offer (with codex win-rates after the first 10 runs), skip for funds.
6. Shop (rounds 4, 8): 3 cards for sale, one remove, one upgrade.
7. Run end: result, rounds, deck, best round replay button, share, retry with same mayor, next district.
8. Daily: countdown, play once, leaderboard, share.
9. Codex: all cards with personal stats; locked cards shown as silhouettes.
10. Settings: audio, haptics, colorblind, reduced motion, language, cloud sync toggle, restore purchases, privacy policy, credits, build number.
11. Unlock: one screen, one price, what you get in 5 bullets, restore link; no countdown timers or fake discounts.

**Input**

- Touch: tap to select and place; long-press on any intersection for an info card; two-finger pinch is intentionally unsupported (fixed zoom).
- Mouse: same as touch plus hover previews.
- Keyboard: 1–5 select cards, arrows move a target cursor, Enter places, Space = Go, Esc = undo/pause. Required for Steam.
- Gamepad: d-pad/stick cursor, A place, B undo, Start = Go. Steam Deck verified-ready layout.

**Onboarding (the first 4 rounds of the first run, fixed draws)**

- Round 1: one intersection, a Smart Signal in hand, a jam forms visibly before Go is even pressed in the preview. One card, one placement, pass.
- Round 2: two cards, introduces One-Way and the demand strip.
- Round 3: a bus round, introduces score values.
- Round 4: first rush round with Detour; fail is possible and teaches approval.
- Tutorial copy is three sentences per round, placed next to the thing it describes, dismissed by doing the thing. No modal walls.
- First 3 runs show a one-line archetype hint on card offers ("FLOW cards work together"). Then never again.

**Accessibility**

- Reduced-motion mode: no camera shake, no trails, instant transitions.
- Colorblind palettes (section 7), text scaling 100–150%, screen-reader labels on all DOM menus, high-contrast toggle.
- No time pressure in placement; the only timed element is the 15-second watch phase, which is pausable.

**Localization**

- All strings in `packages/ui/i18n/*.json` with ICU plurals; card text is generated from templates per language.
- Launch: English. Before mobile launch: German, French, Spanish, Portuguese (BR), Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, Russian, translated by agents, with a native-speaker review pass requested from Idan's network or skipped with a note in the release checklist.
- Store listings localized in the same set; the share card's text version is localized; the emoji strip is universal.

## 9. Monetization and platform integration

**Model: free to try, one purchase.** District 1, two mayors, and 3 days of the daily are free forever. One non-consumable purchase, "Full City," unlocks Districts 2–3, all mayors, endless mode, the daily, run history and replays, and liveries. Steam is premium (the whole game, $11.99) because free-to-try on Steam reads as F2P and depresses reviews.

| Surface | Mechanism | Price (default) | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| iOS | Non-consumable IAP `full_city` | $7.99 tier | RevenueCat Capacitor SDK; StoreKit 2 under the hood; restore button; receipt validated by RevenueCat |
| Android | Non-consumable IAP `full_city` | $7.99 tier | Same RevenueCat integration; Google Play Billing 7 |
| Web (own site, itch) | Stripe Checkout one-time payment | $7.99 | Worker `/license` issues a signed license (Ed25519) bound to a local device UUID; stored locally; optional email for recovery; itch.io also sells a "web unlock key" via itch's own store |
| Poki / CrazyGames | Portal rules: Poki forbids external payments; CrazyGames supports its own IAP | District 1 free; on CrazyGames, unlock via CrazyGames IAP at $7.99; on Poki, the unlock CTA deep-links to the stores and the website | `PortalAdapter` exposes `canSellInPortal()`; the unlock screen adapts |
| Steam | Premium app | $11.99, 10% launch discount | steamworks.js ownership check; achievements (12); Steam Cloud for saves; Deck verified layout |

**Rules**

- One `Entitlement` interface; the game never asks a store directly. Entitlement state is cached locally and re-validated on launch when online.
- The unlock screen appears only when a player taps a locked thing or finishes District 1. It is never a popup.
- Pricing by store tier tables; the team prepares a price table for 40 countries using Apple's and Google's tier equivalents and Steam's recommended regional pricing.
- No promo codes in code; use store-native promo codes.
- Refund-safe: entitlement revocation handled (RevenueCat webhooks → Worker → flag on next launch); Steam handles its own.

- CrazyGames: SDK `sdkGameLoadingStart/Stop`, `gameplayStart/Stop`, `happytime` on district win; user module for optional CrazyGames login (not required); IAP module for the unlock.

**Steam specifics**

## 10. Analytics, telemetry, privacy, backend

**Backend (Cloudflare Workers; all endpoints idempotent, rate-limited, CORS-restricted)**

| Endpoint | Purpose | Storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /daily/:date` | Returns the day's seed, mayor, district (precomputed 365 days ahead by a cron Worker; cached in KV) | KV |
| `POST /leaderboard/:date` | Submit `{handle, score, rounds, timeMs, inputs, finalHash}`; store; sample 5% for re-simulation | D1 |
| `GET /leaderboard/:date?around=rank` | Top 100 plus the window around the player | D1 |

**Analytics events (the only ones; adding one requires an ADR)**

- `app_open`, `run_start {mayor, district, daily}`, `round_end {round, pass, throughput, quota, cardsPlayed[]}`, `card_offered`, `card_picked`, `run_end {rounds, result, deckTags}`, `unlock_viewed {trigger}`, `unlock_purchased {surface}`, `share {kind}`, `settings_changed {key}`, `error {code}`.

**Privacy**

- Privacy policy and terms generated in `docs/legal/` (plain language, listing exactly the data above), hosted on the website, linked from every store listing and the settings screen.
- No ATT prompt on iOS (no tracking); App Privacy label: "Data not linked to you: gameplay content, diagnostics, identifiers (anonymous)". Google Play Data Safety form equivalently.
- COPPA/age: the game is rated for everyone; no chat, no UGC beyond a profanity-filtered leaderboard handle; the handle filter runs client and server side.
- GDPR: a "delete my data" button in settings that calls `DELETE /sync/:uuid` and `DELETE /ingest/:uuid`.

**Operations**

- Everything deploys from CI on tags; Worker secrets via Wrangler; a runbook in `docs/runbooks/` for: rotating the license key, rolling back a daily seed, banning a leaderboard entry, handling a RevenueCat webhook failure.
- Error reporting: a tiny in-house `error` event with a stack hash; no Sentry at launch (keeps the privacy label clean); revisit if crash volume demands.

## 11. Quality gates

**Automated balance simulation (`packages/balance`), the core QA instrument**

- Drafter bots: `random`, `greedy-throughput` (picks the card with the best one-round lookahead), `archetype(tag)` (prefers its tag), `mcts-lite` (3-ply rollout, used as the "skilled player" proxy), and `human-replay` (replays recorded human runs from analytics once available).
- Nightly job: 10,000 runs per district per bot (seeded, parallel across cores; the 2,000 rounds/s budget makes this \~10 minutes on an 8-core runner), producing `docs/balance/latest.md` with: win rate per district and mayor per bot; drafted win-rate delta per card (win rate of runs where the card was drafted minus baseline); pick-rate when offered; archetype purity vs win rate; round-of-death histogram; gridlock frequency; median run length; the top 20 decks.
- Pass criteria (gate the release; a PR that breaks them is blocked): `mcts-lite` win rate on District 1 between 55% and 65%, District 2 between 40% and 50%, District 3 between 25% and 35%; `random` under 10% on District 1; at least 5 archetype bots each above 35% on District 1; no card with drafted delta above +12 or below −8 points; no deck appearing in more than 15% of top-20 lists across seeds; median run length 10–15 minutes at 1× speed.
- Every card PR attaches the delta report for the changed cards. Every quota or demand change attaches the full report.

**Test layers**
