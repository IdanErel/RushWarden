# Name screen: "Gridlock Royale"

_Screened 2026-10-10. Game: a traffic-simulation deckbuilding roguelite (puzzle/strategy) for mobile, web and Steam, sold as a premium unlock with no ads._

## Summary verdict: RISKY. Recommended alternative: **Rushwarden**

**Gridlock Royale is RISKY. It does not collide outright.**

- **What is clear:** nothing is published under the exact title "Gridlock Royale" on any store checked. All four domains (.com, .game, .gg, .io) appear unregistered. There is no live US registration for GRIDLOCK on video game software: the one that existed (Reg. 5569901, class 9) was cancelled in April 2025.
- **Risk 1, a crowded word:** "Gridlock" is very common for games, including traffic-jam puzzles in this exact theme. Examples are _Gridlock: Car Jam Escape Game_ on Google Play, _Gridlock_ on Steam (2024), _Gridlock+_ on the App Store and many itch.io titles. That hurts discoverability and makes the name weak to protect.
- **Risk 2, a pending traffic-themed GRIDLOCK filing:** Board Brain Games has two live, pending US applications, GRIDLOCK (stylized) and GRIDLOCK RULE THE ROAD, for traffic-themed board games in class 28. They are a likely future objector on related goods.
- **Risk 3, "Royale":** I found no evidence that Supercell enforces "ROYALE" on its own. Many "___ Royale" games co-exist, such as Block Royale, Hexa Royale and Riddle Royale. But "Royale" on a card/deck game points straight at _Clash Royale_, which Supercell has registered in class 9. "Royale" also suggests battle-royale PvP, which misdescribes a single-player roguelite.
- **Not checked:** EU (EUIPO/TMview) records could not be retrieved.

**Recommendation: rename to Rushwarden.** It is a coined word, short, and fits the theme ("rush hour" + a warden/commissioner who sets the rules). It had no hits on Steam, the App Store, Google Play, itch.io, Poki or CrazyGames. It had no US trademark records. **rushwarden.com** (RDAP: not found), **rushwarden.game**, **rushwarden.gg** (DNS: does not exist) and **rushwarden.io** (RDAP: not found) all appear free. The runner-up is **Street Edict**. Its rule cards work as "edicts", and its .com and .game appear free.

> Method note: for .com and .io I used registry RDAP (Verisign / Identity Digital); "not found" is strong evidence a domain is unregistered. I verified the method against google.com and google.io, which both returned records. For .game and .gg there was no RDAP I could reach, so I used Google DNS-over-HTTPS. NXDOMAIN there is good but not conclusive, because one RDAP-registered .com (junctioneer.com) also returned NXDOMAIN. Check at a registrar before buying.

## Limits of this search

- **This is not legal advice.** It is a preliminary knockout screen built from public web sources. **Have a trademark attorney run a full clearance search (US, EU/UK and target markets, including common-law and app-store use) before spending money on the name**: logo, domains, store pages, marketing or filings.
- **EU and UK not covered:** EUIPO eSearch and TMview could not be retrieved (errors or robots blocks), and the UK IPO journal was down. EU and UK conflicts remain **unchecked**.
- **US trademark data:** USPTO data came from Justia mirror listings, which can be stale. Key statuses were confirmed on USPTO TSDR. Only word marks were searched. Phonetic equivalents (e.g. "Grid Lock", "Gridlok") and design marks were only partly covered.
- **Store searches:** these were indirect. App Store, Google Play, itch.io, Poki and CrazyGames block direct search fetches, so I used web-search indexes, which can miss new or regional apps. Steam was checked through its store suggest endpoint. SteamDB was not reachable.
- **Domains:** "appears available" means the registry RDAP returned not found (.com, .io) or DNS returned NXDOMAIN (.game, .gg). Premium/reserved pricing and registrar availability were not checked. DNS NXDOMAIN is not conclusive: junctioneer.com is registered but returned NXDOMAIN. Confirm at a registrar.
- **Not assessed:** social handles, YouTube/TikTok names, and non-English meanings.
